How I work

I listen closely. Especially at the point where something shifts.

You can think a problem through a hundred times and still end up in the same place. In the room, I pay attention to the point where something familiar shows up again: pressure, withdrawal, anger, shame, or that feeling of shutting down.

“With Theo, I can start by just talking. Then he asks the right question at the point where I get stuck.”
Anita, 46 · name changed
At the start

We take one moment where it really happened.

There is history behind it: the same argument for the tenth time, closeness has gone, sex feels awkward or far away, jealousy is eating up energy. Or you realise that most of what you do in the relationship is react, and you barely recognise yourself in it.

I let you talk and make a note of the places where something catches. Then we take one scene. The last argument. The moment your body went into high gear. The sentence after which the whole conversation changed.

01 · IN CONVERSATION

We take a real scene.

“You just can’t talk to him” is a place to start. Then I want to know what the conversation looked like last week. Who started? What came back? Where did you feel it turn? There is a lot in those details.

02 · IN THE ROOM

We bring it into the room.

An empty chair, figures, a role switch or a boundary marked out in the room can make something suddenly tangible. You notice quickly what changes in you. It gives us another way into something your head has already gone over a hundred times.

03 · BETWEEN US

You can tell me I’m wrong.

I have ideas about what might be happening and I say them out loud. If you say, “No. That doesn’t fit at all,” we stay with that. Your reaction tells me something. I want to understand what it is like from where you are sitting.

“I like those moments when someone goes quiet for a second and realises: Damn. I really do this every time.”— Theophil Kroller
A few days later

That is where it gets interesting.

Same argument, same tension, same impulse. This time you notice it earlier. You say something before it builds up. You stay in the conversation for another few seconds. Or you set a boundary before you have already gone too far inside.

For me, that is when the work starts to show up in everyday life.

What can happen in a session

What I use depends on the subject and what is happening in the room: conversation, body awareness, role work, figures, models or concrete exercises.
